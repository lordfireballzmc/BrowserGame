# ART.02a — One real Three.js battlefield and imported GLB

**Status:** the first single-scene milestone is implemented and **Chrome-verified** on the opt-in route; the unified scene is now the DEFAULT Warden preview, subject to final branch CI. This is not the completion of the broader ART.02 asset overhaul, nor commercial quality parity.

## What actually changed
- `src/engine/render/UnifiedThreeRenderer.ts` is a **single WebGL2 context on the original gameplay canvas**. The ground/cliffs, authored road, 10 pads, northern river and bridges, 258 seeded props, Warden, all deterministic marching Scrappers/Runners, copper projectile meshes and contact glints now use indexed Three.js `BufferGeometry` and cached `InstancedMesh` pools. There is no second compositing canvas, unrelated camera, or separately driven asset animation loop.
- Author-authored palette-slot overrides survive: original linear-light per-vertex color ramps and per-instance fallback tokens become `vertexColors` on `MeshStandardMaterial`, with warm directional light, cool hemisphere fill, shadow receivers and a 2048px PCF directional sun atlas. `toThreeGeometry()` is unit-tested for identical positions, normals, indices and exact sRGB→linear palette values.
- The exact same **702KB pinned CC0 Lantern GLB** from ART.01 loads through Three `GLTFLoader` directly into the scene alongside the other bodies, so it shares their **actual color/depth render target and directional shadow map**. The model is not a billboard or a runtime remote CDN request. Position is sampled from the game's existing terrain height; rigging and texture quality of the rest of the game remain separate work.
- The existing `OrthoCamera` projection/view matrices are copied each frame; only the same authoritative 60Hz Warden, enemy and bolt simulation is used. With normal gameplay, all objects share the same camera, depth and directional lighting, unlike the previous opt-in two-WebGL2-context overlay.
- `?view=movement` is now the primary unified Three.js prototype. `?view=movement&renderer=legacy` explicitly retains a testable **legacy fallback**. Full map inspection and cinematic art views still use the previous renderer. The old `ImportedArtLayer.ts` two-canvas implementation was deleted, and its duplicate second context no longer exists.
- This is a **migration of authored old meshes into a professional rendering engine**, NOT a claim of new AAA characters, a completed animation rig, extra tower content or a new mobile release. The clear goal of ART.02b is to migrate all remaining map/cinematic tooling and validate one renderer before deleting the entire custom rendering backend.

## Still visibly below the user's required Kingdom Rush-inspired 3D style
1. **Character quality:** Warden, Scrapper and Runner remain procedurally blocky with rigid mesh bob/scale; no skeletal locomotion, foot contact, expressive face, anticipation, or polished recoil.
2. **Architecture/ground:** pad bases are plain grey disks; keep roof and beams remain too simple, and the keep crops at the lower edge in landscape/portrait. Grass is flat, with scattered stock-like triangles rather than artist-directed clumps/material ramps.
3. **Shading:** more pronounced shadows help depth but lighting/color are not yet art-directed professionally. Some contact transitions are hard, many surfaces lack painterly texture and tonal variation, and materials do not yet match a cohesive illustrated fantasy asset family.
4. **Motion/UI:** only small fixed-rate whole-mesh motion and minimal physical bolt effects; HUD is still an engineering diagnostic, not tactile touch-first fantasy UI.
5. **Runtime:** no real mobile hardware measurements, 30 FPS minimum proof, sustained GPU time, texture memory profile or verified medium-quality downgrade. The 2048px sun map is an unoptimized initial upper-quality budget subject to profiling.

## 1. Fundamental visual identity

A 16:9 widescreen screenshot of a fictional indie fantasy tower-defense game, illustrated in a **hand-painted, angular, storybook strategy-game art style** that combines the dimensional composition of an isometric game environment with the visible brushwork, simplified forms, graphic shadows, and deliberately imperfect edges of a traditional digital painting.

**The image must resemble a playable game scene painted by an experienced environment and character artist, not a photograph, polished 3D render, generic mobile-game advertisement, or cinematic fantasy concept painting.**

The visual language is built around:

- Large, sharply defined painted shapes with irregular, hand-cut-looking edges.
- Simplified three-dimensional geometry represented through broad blocks of color rather than smooth material shading.
- Visible directional brushstrokes, especially in foliage, dirt, stone, wood, and distant cliffs.
- Slightly rough, layered paint textures that resemble dry-brush acrylic or opaque gouache applied digitally.
- Angular planes of light and shadow, with relatively abrupt transitions between values.
- Deliberately stylized character anatomy, with oversized heads, compact torsos, short limbs, and exaggerated combat equipment.
- Restrained saturation, with warm ochre and olive-green surroundings contrasted against deep teal shadows, blue defenders, crimson enemies, and small concentrated orange and cyan magical effects.
- A cohesive, slightly weathered fantasy aesthetic that carries through the environment, characters, towers, banners, and interface.

The visual style is **painterly 2.5D indie-game illustration**. All objects have believable dimensional form, but their surfaces remain visibly illustrated. Nothing should look physically rendered, plastic, shiny, airbrushed, or procedurally generated.

## 7. Surface treatment and brushwork

Every object must use the same illustrative rendering approach.

Broad brush-painted color planes define volume. Texture is concentrated along plane transitions and within selected material surfaces.

Stone has scratched, chipped, uneven planes. Wood contains long directional streaks following its grain. Leaves are created with irregular angular daubs and overlapping color patches. Dirt is painted with warm, broken horizontal strokes.

Subtle canvas-like grain and dry-brush texture are visible throughout the entire image, including the sky and background mountains.

Edges vary intentionally. Important silhouettes are sharp and high-contrast; less important environmental features have slightly broken, painterly edges.

There are no hard black comic-book outlines. Objects separate through color contrast, overlapping shapes, and painted shadow regions.

Lighting comes predominantly from the upper-left, with warm highlights along exposed stone, foliage, and characters. Shadows are stylized into broad, darker graphic shapes with little soft-gradient blending.

The final surface should resemble a professionally illustrated indie-game screenshot painted over carefully constructed geometric forms.
